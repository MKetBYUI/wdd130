AZ Solar History

Provide relevant, consumer-facing information about privately owned, residential solar systems, that can interconnect with Arizona’s major electric utility providers: APS, SRP, and TEP. These providers do a lot more than distribute power up and down your street, they also generate electricity for local use, as well as Arizona’s participation in the Western Area Power Administration (WAPA) grid, a multi-state grid coordination/sychronization system. Which is also part of the Western Area Coordination Council (WECC), that extends all the way into Western Canada.

With that said, You can still Empower our roof - But First - You need to understand some politi-solar-nuance Here's the catch: How much solar you can install, and how an interconnected system is limited to perform financially, and conform functionally inside a draconian rate plan, drives a limited-choices monopoly-bus rigtht through your home using topology-specific policies and pricing mandates, which by design can drive down your bottom line annual savings to net-zero, or worse. That bottom-line variable continues to erode and devolve in the wake of an ongoing dumpster fire that the ACC has never been able to get under control. Hence, your partial energy "independence" continues to be mostly "dependent" upon your provider’s benevolence, cooperation, internal politics and supervisory over-reach. Which can include additional spy-meter-visibility behind their billing meter, that is ever-aware of YOUR internal storage-capacity as well as being able to mathematically interpret your private, internal usage behaviors.

Solar was originally designed to be very simple: Roll back to the Obama years of 2009-2010 when an industry and concept was spawned: You produce a seasonally predictable amount of electricity on your roof, use what you need while the sun is shining and the A/C is running, and then "share" what you don’t need back through your meter, to the grid provider's "secondary" street-side-wiring, for a very short electrical hop down or across the street, literally at the speed-of-light, where the serving utility instantly gratifies itself by re-selling all of your pass-back energy through any other neighbor's billing meter that is always spinning forward "somewhere" in the cul-de-sac, All done with a reasonable net benefit for the provider because they did not have to do anything to generate that excess power themselves. All they had to do is "exist" with the wires already buried or strung across the street. Maybe your excess, unused energy helps a neighbor do laundry, or is keeping the stay-at-home parent and the kids across the street cool while you're at work.
In theory it is an elegantly simple exchange that should be satisfactory to all parties involved.
In practice, Not so much? FFWD a few years to 2013 - 2014: Behind the scenes at electric utilities there is a degree of anxiousness and maybe even panic over the velocity, quality and quantity of conversion to private renewables that is taking place. Higher connection fees, layers of taxes and surcharges by some utilities that don't exist at others, export limitations, or straight-up denials of reasonable compensation, auto-diminishing buy-back tariffs, intelligent spy-meter placements behind the utility billing meters. All designed toward anti-competiton, anti-sustainable plans to incluide commercial billiing concepts like punitive demand charges. All designed to abolish a previously simple and elegaqnt concept and create a confusing energy-maze nightmare for the consumer. De-centralization of the energy production with a "sustainable" approach toward our home planet was back on a shelf where it sits today.

Arizona’s backyard, hometown energy scene is revenue-profitability-driven by three major utility companies: APS-Pinnacle West, SRP, and TEP-Unisource. These entities don’t just deliver power down the street and into your home, >Most, but not all of Arizona's energy delivery entities are overseen and marginally manageed by the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC). Others, like SRP, follow their own legacy governance models that are generally antiquated and operate outside the ACC reugulatory environment. As an example: The supposedly Not-For-Profit entity SRP, has internal politics and internal policy-driven solar-rate deployments that are blatantly punitive in nature and should effectively discourage any intelligent, renewable-adopter from engaging any alternative (competitive) energy management structure that is not driving 100% of their energy expendature back toward SRP. Historical SRP rate plan manipulation behavior "quacks just like a corporate duck" and uses the duck-curve hysteria rehtoric to justify their obsession with "revenue preservation" which is quasi-government-entity code for "profitability". Internal SRP memo disclosures allegedly brag on how lucrative their favorite solar rate plan is, as they have plenty of ammenity type facilities that are unrelated to production, distribution, delivery or maintenance and can be used as shock-absorbers for extra solar-plan revenue that should probably be returned to their so-called members each year.

What does this mean for you? Whether you're just starting your solar analysis or rethinking past purchase decisions, understanding Solar Rates, Policies, and Politics (SRPP) is essential to making Smart Energy Management (SEM) decisions.
