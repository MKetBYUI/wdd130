Overview

Before You Invest, Knowledge is only potential power. It becomes kinetic energy when applied—when you make smart, informed decisions.
With that said, yes, you can still empower your roof. But first, you need to understand the politi-solar nuance. And yes, there's a catch: how much solar you're allowed to install, how your system is interconnected, and how it's forced to conform to draconian rate plans—all of it is dictated by a monopoly-driven framework. These topology-specific policies and pricing mandates can drive your annual savings down to net-zero—or worse.

This isn't just a theoretical risk. It's a real, ongoing erosion of value, fueled by a regulatory dumpster fire the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC) has never managed to extinguish. Your so-called energy “independence” remains largely dependent on your provider's benevolence, internal politics, and supervisory overreach. That includes the use of advanced “spy meters” behind your billing meter—devices that can monitor your storage capacity and interpret your private usage behaviors with unsettling precision.

Solar Was Supposed to Be Simple, Reliable and Economical: Back in 2009-2010, solar was elegantly straightforward. You produced a predictable amount of electricity on your roof, used what you needed while the sun was shining, and shared the excess with your neighbors via the grid. That excess energy traveled a short hop—literally at the speed of light—down the street, where it was instantly resold by your utility through someone else's billing meter. The utility didn't generate that power. They didn't even move it very far. They just existed—with wires already in place—and profited from your surplus.

Maybe your unused energy helped a neighbor do laundry or kept a stay-at-home parent and their kids cool while you were at work. It is a clean, efficient seamless exchange. Everyone benefits.
Fast forward to 2013-2014. Utilities began to panic over the rapid adoption of private renewables. Behind the scenes, they scrambled to preserve revenue. The net result? Higher connection fees. More charges and surcharges. Export limitations. Denials of fair compensation. Diminishing buyback tariffs. And yes, those solar-spy smart-meters that track your intake toward storage and discharge usage.

Utilities began applying commercial tactics that track lower level demand charges of less than 10kW and pushing punitive charges toward residential solar customers. The goal was clear: encumber and inhibit the simple system processing and replace it with a confusing, anti-competitive, revenue recovery maze. The concept of decentralizing sustainable solar energy was put back on the shelf. Don't misunderstand; utility companies value solar, just not you your roof.

Arizona's Energy Landscape is a Profitability-Driven model and is dominated by three major players: APS (Pinnacle West), SRP, and TEP (UniSource). These aren't just power producers and distributors, they are energy policy architects that keep tabs on what the other providers are getting away with. Most are nominally overseen by the AZCC, but SRP operates under its own antiquated governance model, entirely outside ACC visibility, reporting or jurisdiction.

SRP, a psuedo-municipal "not-for-profit" entity, has deployed solar rate plans so punitive they should deter any rational prospect from pursuing sustainable alternative energy. Their internal politics and policy decisions are designed to funnel 100% of your energy expenditure back into their system whether they centrally produced the power, or not. SRP's solar customer billing behaviors and revenue processing “quack like a corporate duck.” They invoke the infamous “duck curve” to justify their obsession with revenue enhancements, which is code for "profitability". Internal memos allegedly boast about how lucrative their favorite demand-focused solar rate plan is. Surplus revenue is easily absorbed by employee amenity facilities unrelated to energy production, transmission, maintenance, or delivery. Excess revenues could probably be refunded annualy, to so-called members if transparent reconciliation excluded expendatures on non-energy related line items.

- Arizona Public Service -

The Resource Comparison Proxy is designed to reflect the value of solar energy through the grid, based on avoided costs such as fuel and infrastructure. While it offers a more favorable export rate than SRP, it still falls short of retail value, making strategic energy use essential for solar customers What This Means for APS Solar Customers To get the most out of your solar investment in APS territory, consider the following strategies: Shift high-energy activities (laundry, dishwashing, EV charging) to off-peak hours when rates are lower Use programmable thermostats and smart appliances to align usage with solar production Consider battery storage to reduce reliance on grid power during peak pricing windows APS's structure is more solar-friendly than SRP's, but still requires thoughtful planning to optimize return on investment Customers who actively manage their energy use can still achieve strong long-term savings.




- Salt River Project -

SRP's unique structure dates back to the early 1900s, when large landowners pledged property to secure federal water projects. Today, that feudal caste system legacy continues through a dual-board system where voting power influence is delegated out according to land ownership. This has led to rate plans and pricing policies that favor large agricultural land-owners and some legacy commercial users over residential solar-serf members What This Means for SRP Solar Members Installing solar in SRP territory requires a strategic approach. Because of the low export rate and high demand charges, maximizing self-consumption is critical. This usualy means: Investing in energy storage systems, in order to shift solar energy access into optimal (5:PM for dinner) time-of-use demand windows Using smart home technology and control products that manage peak consumption spikes and avoid the associated demand charges Adjusting lifestyle habits and activities to strategically align with off-peak consumption and solar power production While these strategies can help, they also require some heafty upfront capital and energy management focus if you are going-solar. For most SRP members, their financial impact and return will take significantly longer to realize than an APS or TEP customer with similar energy requirements.

Images for the Page "SRP"




- Tucson Electric Power -

TEP's Approach to Solar TEP has taken a more measured approach to distributed solar compared to SRP or APS, offering a moderately competitive export rate and fewer punitive charges However, the utility still prioritizes grid stability and cost recovery, which means solar customers must be strategic to maximize savings What This Means for TEP Solar Customers To make the most of your solar investment in TEP territory, consider the following: Design your system to closely match your household's daytime energy usage Use smart appliances and timers to shift usage into solar production hours Evaluate whether a battery storage expense is worth reducing some peak-hour grid reliance TEP's structure offers way more flexibility than SRP's draconian approach and a slightly higher export rate than APS. It is always cheaper to "reduce" than it is to "produce", so with thoughtful planning, solar TEP customers can probably achieve a decent return time on their investment, especially when paired with energy-efficient habits and technologies.
