Empower Your Roof - But First - Understand Arizona's Political Solar Nuances

Thinking of going solar in Arizona? You're not alone and you are no longer navigating the solar maze alone. Homeowners across the country are considering solar to ease their energy expense line, and reduce their always-dirty consumer-power footprint, only to find out that going-simple-solar has evolved into a beast that saves the consumer-client very little, compared to a relatively large investment. Here's the catch: How much solar you can install, and how an interconnected system is required to conform, drives your bus through solar price plans and topology-policy mandates, which drives your bottom line. That bottom-line variable continues to evolve and is historically dependent on your provider’s cooperation, politics and spy-meter visibility into YOUR internal system performance and processes.

Solar was originally designed to be simple: You produce some predictable amount of electricity on your roof, use what you need while the sun is shining, and share what you don’t need back through your meter, to the grid provider's street-side wiring, for a very short electrical hop down or across the street, literally at the speed-of-light, where the serving utility instantly RE-SELLs it ALL back through your neighbor's billing meter that is spinning in a forward direction, (all done with a reasonable markup and at ZERO additional infrastructure cost to the serving utility). Maybe your excess, unused energy helps a neighbor do laundry, or is keeping the stay-at-home parent and the kids cool while you're at work.
In theory it is an elegantly simple exchange.
In practice? Well, it's a virtual rat maze, re-purposed to be a punitive consumer maze. Higher connection fees, layers of taxes and surcharges, export limitations, or denials of reasonable compensation, auto-diminishing buy-back tariffs, intelligent spy-meter placements and unsustainable, punitive, commercial-style demand charges often apply to a previously simple concept. All of which has become a punitive financial maze for going "sustainable". Essntially, the utility that owns exclusive coverage of your street and Zip Code drives the bus and dictates the rules.

Arizona’s energy scene is shaped by three utility giants: APS, SRP, and TEP-Unisource. These entities don’t just deliver power to your home, they generate massive amounts of constant, and on-demand energy using various sources that are embedded in the grid known as WAPA. That entity coordinates energy production, delivery and stability across several western states. Some providers are overseen and marginally manageed by the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC), while others, like SRP, follow their own governance models that are entirely outside the ACC’s jurisdiction. (That is another subject matter discussion in and of itself). As an example: The supposedly Not-For-Profit entity SRP, has internal politics and regard toward privately owned solar which is less than benevolent, or even tollerable. Their rate plan modification behavior looks to be overly-obsessed with profitability. Internally they have plenty of ammenity type facilities that are unrelated to production, distribution or delivery and can be used as shock absorbers for extra fees, charges and revenue that should probably be returned to their members each year.

What does this mean for you? Whether you're just starting your solar analysis or rethinking past purchase decisions, understanding Solar Rates, Policies, and Politics (SRPP) is essential to making Smart Energy Management (SEM) decisions.

This site is designed to provide clear, actionable insight that is consumer-first guidance toward empowering SEM.

Salt River Project "SRP" is the one of the oldest and is the 2nd largest power producer in Arizona.
