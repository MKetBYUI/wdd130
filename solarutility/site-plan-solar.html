Overview

Purpose

Provide relevant, consumer-facing information about privately owned, residential solar systems, that can interconnect with Arizona’s major electric utility providers: APS, SRP, and TEP. These providers do a lot more than distribute power up and down your street, they also generate electricity for local use, as well as Arizona’s participation in the Western Area Power Administration (WAPA) grid, a multi-state grid coordination/sychronization system. Which is also part of the Western Area Coordination Council (WECC), that extends all the way into Western Canada.

Thinking of going solar in Arizona? You're no longer alone as you try to understand and navigate the solar-energy utility-maze. Homeowners across the state continue to consider solar as an option to ease up on their energy consumption expense line, and also reduce their dirty-consumer-power footprint, only to find out that simple-solar-solution expectations have devolved into a storage-timing and energy-management nightmare that serves their neighborhood utility best, but saves the consumer-client little-to-nothing, compared to a relatively large investment.
Here's the catch: How much solar you can install, and how an interconnected system is limited to perform financially, and conform functionally inside a draconian rate plan, drives a limited-choices monopoly-bus rigtht through your home using topology-specific policies and pricing mandates, which by design can drive down your bottom line annual savings to net-zero, or worse. That bottom-line variable continues to erode and devolve in the wake of an ongoing dumpster fire that the ACC has never been able to get under control. Hence, your partial energy "independence" continues to be mostly "dependent" upon your provider’s benevolence, cooperation, internal politics and supervisory over-reach. Which can include additional spy-meter-visibility behind their billing meter, that is ever-aware of YOUR internal storage-capacity as well as being able to mathematically interpret your private, internal usage behaviors.

Solar was originally designed to be very simple: Roll back to the Obama years of 2009-2010 when an industry and concept was spawned: You produce a seasonally predictable amount of electricity on your roof, use what you need while the sun is shining and the A/C is running, and then "share" what you don’t need back through your meter, to the grid provider's "secondary" street-side-wiring, for a very short electrical hop down or across the street, literally at the speed-of-light, where the serving utility instantly gratifies itself by re-selling all of your pass-back energy through any other neighbor's billing meter that is always spinning forward "somewhere" in the cul-de-sac, All done with a reasonable net benefit for the provider because they did not have to do anything to generate that excess power themselves. All they had to do is "exist" with the wires already buried or strung across the street. Maybe your excess, unused energy helped a neighbor do laundry, or is keeping the stay-at-home parent and the kids across the street cool while you're at work.
In theory it is an elegantly simple exchange that should be satisfactory to all parties involved.
In practice, Not so much? FFWD a few years to 2013 - 2014: Behind the scenes at electric utilities there is a degree of anxiousness and maybe even panic over the velocity, quality and quantity of conversion to private renewables that is taking place. Leasing companies have been popping us all over the place as finance vehicles and as signficant "competitors" to the utility comanpies that are used to their private-spaces of non-competitive environments. The astute and crafty leasing companies spotted the huge opportunities available to capitalize on the ITC and the associated tax credits that were fundamentally intended to provide incentive as tax relief to the end-user-homeowner. Back at Utility Ranch Headquarters, a virtual rat maze is in re-development by some very smart think-tank types, some with PHD's. The maze, or puzzle if you will, is being re-configured to be an effective consumer maze. Higher connection fees, layers of taxes and surcharges by some utilities that don't exist at others, export limitations, or straight-up denials of reasonable compensation, auto-diminishing buy-back tariffs, intelligent spy-meter placements behind the utility billing meters. All designed toward anti-competiton, anti-sustainable plans to incluide commercial billiing concepts like punitive demand charges. All designed to abolish a previously simple and elegaqnt concept and create a confusing energy-maze nightmare for the consumer. De-centralization of the energy production with a "sustainable" approach toward our home planet was back on a shelf where it sits today.

Arizona’s backyard, hometown energy scene is revenue-profitability-driven by three major utility companies: APS-Pinnacle West, SRP, and TEP-Unisource. These entities don’t just deliver power down the street and into your home, they also generate significant amounts of constant on-demand and variable peak-energy, using various sources that are also synced into the wide-area-network grid known as WAPA which is a federal organiaztion under the US Dept of Energy. That Western Area Power Association is federal for a reason and is essential, in that it was set up in 1977 to coordinate mostly hydropower from 57 dam projects, as well as other genaration sources covered by the Buereau of Reclamation. With that said, WAPA's primary mission is NOT to ensure delivery of stable, reliable power to your home, it is to ensure stable, reliable delivery of contractual power to projects that fall into "the greater good category". A lot of that energy and resouce management goes into retaining, managing and moving WATER resources that keep all of us hydrated and fed. (Central Arizona Project, as an example) Olny excess energy that may be left over from essential contractual projects, gets liquidated back via WAPA transmission/transfer/balancing, toward delivery players like Pinnacle West, Unisource and SRP. Those transactions and agreements are probably done with some type of zero-visibility "spot market" purchase-price scenario. For this reason the serving utilities must finance and maintain resources like Peaker Plants that ramp up, and then back down to strategically meet city-wide system "demands" like 1,000,000 AC units spinning back up between 4:PM and 8:PM.

Most, but not all of Arizona's energy delivery entities are overseen and marginally manageed by the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC). Others, like SRP, follow their own legacy governance models that are generally antiquated and operate outside the ACC reugulatory environment. As an example: The supposedly Not-For-Profit entity SRP, has internal politics and internal policy-driven solar-rate deployments that are blatantly punitive in nature and should effectively discourage any intelligent, renewable-adopter from engaging any alternative (competitive) energy management structure that is not driving 100% of their energy expendature back toward SRP. Historical SRP rate plan manipulation behavior "quacks just like a corporate duck" and uses the duck-curve hysteria rehtoric to justify their obsession with "revenue preservation" which is quasi-government-entity code for "profitability". Internal SRP memo disclosures allegedly brag on how lucrative their favorite solar rate plan is, as they have plenty of ammenity type facilities that are unrelated to production, distribution, delivery or maintenance and can be used as shock-absorbers for extra solar-plan revenue that should probably be returned to their so-called members each year.

What does this mean for you? Whether you're just starting your solar analysis or rethinking past purchase decisions, understanding Solar Rates, Policies, and Politics (SRPP) is essential to making Smart Energy Management (SEM) decisions.

This site is designed to provide clear, concise, correct insights, as consumer-empowering guidance, toward applying SEM to their bottom line.
