Overview

Purpose

Provide relevant, consumer-facing information about privately owned residential solar systems that interconnect with Arizona’s major utilities: APS, SRP, and TEP. These providers do more than distribute power—they also generate electricity for Arizona’s role in the Western Area Power Administration (WAPA), a multi-state grid coordination system.

Empower Your Roof - But First - Understand Arizona Solar Nuances

Thinking of going solar in Arizona? You're not alone—and you're no longer navigating it alone. Homeowners across the state are turning to solar to ease energy bills and reduce their dirty-consumer-power footprint.

But there’s a catch: how much solar you can install—and how your system connects to the grid—depends entirely on your utility provider’s requirements.

Arizona’s energy scene is shaped by three utility giants: APS, SRP, and TEP/Unisource. These entities don’t just deliver power—they generate their own and are deeply embedded in the WAPA grid, coordinating energy across western states.

Solar should be simple: produce electricity on your roof, use what you need, and share what you don’t with the grid. Maybe your extra power helps a neighbor do laundry while you're at work. In theory, it’s elegant.

In practice? It’s a maze. Connection fees, surcharges, taxes, export limits, financial penalties—and sometimes, even commercial-style demand charges—can apply. The utility that covers your ZIP code dictates the rules.

Some providers are overseen by the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC), while others, like SRP, follow their own governance—entirely outside the ACC’s jurisdiction.

What does this mean for you? Whether you're just starting your solar journey or revisiting past decisions, knowing your utility’s rates, policies, and politics is essential to making a smart investment.

This site is designed to provide clear, actionable insight—consumer-first guidance that empowers your energy choices.

Before you buy—start here. Knowledge really is power. Let’s build an energy future your grandchildren can be proud of.

Audience

AZ electric utility customers exploring photovoltaic power who want to understand the application, deployment, and tariff-related challenges of self-produced solar electricity.

Salt River Project "SRP" is the one of the oldest and is the 2nd largest power producer in Arizona.

SRP is allegedly a "Not For Profit" entity that was founded at the turn of the 20th Century as a flood control and irrigation water system developer. SRP is still an un-regulated pseudo-municipality and some member-customers have limited voting rights while most residential members do not.
