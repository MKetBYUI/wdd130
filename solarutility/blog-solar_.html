AZ Solar History Overview

Just because conspiracy and collusion are subtle and quiet, it doesn't mean it is not going on. Those two concepts by their very nature are behind the scenes and clandestine. Arizona's Energy Landscape is a Profitability-Driven model and is dominated by three major players: APS (Pinnacle West), TEP (UniSource) and of-course SRP. These aren't just power producers and distributors, they are energy policy architects that keep tabs on what the other providers are getting away with. Most are nominally overseen by the AZCC, but SRP along with other NFP's, operate under their own antiquated governance models, entirely outside ACC visibility, reporting or jurisdiction.

Before You Invest:
Knowledge only has potential power. It becomes kinetic energy that performs real work when applied by making informed decisions.
With that said, Yes, you can still empower your roof. But first, you need to understand the politi-solar nuances in your area of jurisdiction, And of course there's a catch: How much solar you're allowed to install, how your system is interconnected, and how it needs to perform within a draconian rate plan is managed by a non-competitive, monopoly-driven framework. These topology-specific policies and pricing mandates can drive your annual savings down to net-zero—or worse.

This isn't just a theoretical maybe. It's a real, ongoing erosion of value, fueled via a regulatory dumpster fire that the Arizona Corporation Commission has never managed to get under control. Not withstanding the outliers like SRP, your potential for any level of energy independence, or minimized savings remains largely dependent on your provider's benevolence, internal politics and behind-the-meter monitoring that preserves their overreach. This typically includes the application of smart “spy meters”, behind their billing meter, that actually monitor your storage capacity and can be used to interpret private usage behaviors with unsettling precision. As an example of how the system works against you: Your ability to purchase fuel (stored energy) for your car would be restricted to a single brand of fuel, based on your address, and there would be a remote telemetry device (monitor) on your car that keeps track of your rates of acceleration, and precisely keeps tabs of how fast your driving habits are depleting your fuel tank, as you accelerate from 0 mph up to 45 mph around town. Would you put up with that scenario in real life? In fact, SRP actually produced and posted a ridiculous video a few years ago that typified your driving (energy) habits as a NASCAR driver, with a helmet and a crash cage tearing through town instead of maintaining a reasonable and prudent speed. In other words, non-solar customers can accelerate and decelerate with impunity, while solar adopters must maintain school zone speeds on their daily commute to the office.

Solar Was Supposed to Be Simple, Reliable and Economical: Back in 2009-2010, the original solar concept was elegantly straightforward. You produced a seasonally predictable amount of electricity on your roof. More during the longer days of summer, less in the shorter days of winter. Your home consumed what was needed while the sun was shining, and the excess went over to your neighbors place via the utility company street wiring. That excess energy traveled a very short hop in terms of utility distances, literally at the speed of light, where it was instantly resold by your friendly neighborhood single-source utility company, through their bi-directional smart meter on someone else's house. No harm, No foul. The utility didn't generate that power. They didn't even move it very far. They just managed it by default with wires already buried in place that handled the relatively small amount of surplus. It was a wash for them, and therein lies their problem.

Maybe your unused energy helped a neighbor heat some water, do some laundry, or kept the stay-at-home parent and the kids cool while you were at work. It was a clean, efficient seamless exchange. Everyone benefits....
The utility company has less demand on their mid-day production resources, as they move (resell) your excess energy across the street, to which they had no expense or effort in relation to the production, nor increased expenses in transportation.

Fast forward to 2013-2014. Utilities are in full panic mode over the accelerating deployment of the private solar ownership model. Behind the scenes they are screaming hysterically about the "Duck Curve", pointing to the mess that massive solar adoption in Hawaii was causing for their antiquated, unkept and overworked grid structure that still has WW2 cobwebs hanging off of their under-equipped slumlord system. Meanwhile, they are strategizing rhetoric in their back-offices and lobbying at the AZCC to preserve declining revenue. The net lobbying result: Full ignition of an AZCC dumpster fire. Higher "connection fees", more charges and surcharges, export limitations, diminishing buyback tariffs, and more recently there are outright denials of any compensation with some electric delivery providers. And don't forget, those smart-spy-meter deployments that track energy intake going toward your private storage container and your corresponding energy usage.

Utilities can and do apply punitive commercial tactics that track lower level demand charges at even less than 10kW, and then leverage those exclusively punitive charges toward residential customers who dare to go solar. The goal is clear: Discourage access, encumber and inhibit the simple system processing and replace it with a confusing, anti-competitive, revenue recovery maze. The concept of decentralized, sustainable solar energy savings is done. If not permanently "deep six'd", it is for sure on a back shelf. Please don't misunderstand the previous dialog; utility companies absolutely value solar, they just love to hate it on your roof.

SRP, a pseudo-municipal-NFP "not-for-profit" entity, has deployed solar rate plans so punitive they should deter any intelligent, rational solar prospect from pursuing sustainable energy alternatives. Their internal politics and policy decisions are designed to funnel 100% of your energy expenditure back into their system whether they centrally produced the power, or not. SRP's solar customer billing behaviors and revenue processing “quack like a corporate duck.”. They helped propagate the infamous “duck curve” hysteria based on the technical difficulties that Hawaiian slum-lord utilities were having with their 1950's architecture, in order to justify their obsession with revenue retention, which is NFP code for an accounting principle known as "profitability". Internal memos allegedly boast about how lucrative their favorite demand-focused solar rate plan is. Surplus revenue is easily absorbed by SRP for some very nice amenity facilities that are completely unrelated to energy production, transmission, maintenance, or delivery. Excess revenues could/should probably be refunded annually to so-called members, if transparent reconciliation highlighted internal expenditures on non-energy line items.

What does this mean for you? Whether you're just starting your solar analysis or rethinking past purchase decisions, understanding Solar Rates, Policies, and Politics (SRPP) is essential to making Smart Energy Management (SEM) decisions.
