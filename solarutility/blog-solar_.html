AZ Solar History Overview

While conspiracy, anti-trust and collusion are usually subtle, concealed and quiet, it doesn't mean that nothing is going on behind you. Those concepts by their very nature are behind the scenes and clandestine. Arizona's Energy Landscape is influenced by a revenue-profitability-driven model that is dominated by three major players: Pinnacle West (APS), UniSource (TEP) and of-course NFP-SRP. These aren't just power producers and distributors, they are energy policy architects that keep close tabs on what the other non-competitive providers are getting away with. Most of the non-competitive energy producers and resellers in Arizona are nominally monitored and regulated by the AZCC, but SRP along with other not-for-profit NFP's operate "behind" antiquated governance models, entirely outside of AZCC visibility (reporting) or jurisdiction.

Solar Was Supposed to Be Simple, Reliable and Economical: Back in 2009 - 2010, the original solar concept was elegantly straightforward. You produced a seasonally predictable amount of electricity on your roof. More during the longer days of summer, less in the shorter days of winter. You set your thermostat at a preferred, comfortable temperature for your home and left for work. Most systems in the typical home auto-cycle, that is to say that they cycle On and Off. Things like heat pumps, pool pumps and water heaters could cycle On and would get some good offset contribution from solar. That concept also includes refrigerators and freezers. Solar also covers continuous low-energy parasite loads like appliance clocks, network routers, garage door openers and device chargers that are always ready to consume all of the renewable energy they needed while the sun was shining. Any incidental excess energy went backward through the bi-directional billing meter and slid over to the neighbors billing-meter, via the utility secondary street wiring. Maybe your unused energy helped a neighbor heat some water, cook some food, do some laundry, or kept the non-typical stay-at-home parent and the kids cool while you were at work. It was a clean, efficient, seamless exchange. Everyone benefited.
The utility company had less demand on their mid-day production resources, as they moved (resold) your (free to them) excess energy across the street. To which they had zero expense or effort in relation to the production, transmission, or expense of transfer. That excess energy traveled a very short hop in terms of utility distances, literally at the speed of light, where it was instantly resold by your friendly, neighborhood, single-source utility company. It went right through their bi-directional smart meter into someone else's house and AC unit. No harm, No foul. The utility didn't generate that power. They didn't even move it very far. They just managed it organically, with wires already buried in place that handled your relatively small amount of energy surplus without additional expense nor effort. But that transfer was only a wash for them. (Therein lies the root "dumpster fire" problem).

Fast forward to 2013-2014. Utilities are in full revenue panic mode over the accelerating deployment of the decentralizing, private-energy-production model. Behind the scenes and on public platforms they are screaming hysterically about the "Duck Curve! the Duck Curve!", while pointing to the hot-mess that massive solar adoption in Hawaii was causing for their antiquated, unkept and overworked grid structure that still had some WW2 cobwebs hanging off of their under-equipped, low-tech, slumlord system. Meanwhile, Arizona utility companies are commiserating, strategizing and developing more rhetoric in their back-offices, while soliciting the AZCC to stop the bleeding and to help recover diverted revenue. The net utility outcry result was Full Ignition of the AZCC Solar Dumpster Fire. Higher "connection fees", more charges, more surcharges and weird "taxes" that no-one outside of Energy HQ understands, system limitations, export limitations, diminishing buyback tariffs, and more recently there are outright denials of any net-energy compensation with various electric delivery providers. And don't forget, those smart-spy-meter deployments that track energy intake going from YOUR solar, into YOUR privately owned storage container. Which are inherently providing data visibility that can help quantify your corresponding energy time-window habits, which is essential data that can be used toward developing future rate plan strategies.

Utilities can and do apply punitive commercial tactics that track lower level demand charges at even less than 10kW, (that "10kW" is about the normal equivalent of your dryer and your AC unit running at the same time). That demand data is then leveraged to inflict punitive demand charges against residential customers who dare to go solar. The goals are very clear: Discourage access, Encumber and Inhibit all simple-system processing, to be thoughtfully replaced with a hyper-confusing, anti-competitive, Revenue Recovery Rat Maze (RRRM). The concept of decentralized, sustainable, solar energy savings is done. If not permanently "Deep Six'd", it is for sure locked up in a back-room wall-safe.

Please don't misunderstand. Utility companies absolutely understand the value solar energy, but they also Love to Hate solar energy production on YOUR roof. SRP, a pseudo-municipal-NFP "not-for-profit" entity, has deployed solar rate plans so punitive they should deter any intelligent, rational solar prospect from pursuing sustainable energy alternatives. Their internal politics and policy decisions are designed to reflect 100% of your energy expenditure dollars back-around and into their system whether they centrally produced the power, or not. SRP's solar customer billing behaviors and revenue processing “quack like a corporate duck.”. They helped propagate the infamous “duck curve” hysteria based on the technical difficulties that Hawaiian slum-lord utilities were having with their 1950's architecture, in order to justify their obsession with revenue retention, which is NFP code for an accounting principle known as "profitability". Internal memos allegedly boast about how lucrative their favorite demand-based solar rate plan is. Surplus revenue is easily absorbed by SRP for some very nice employee amenity facilities that are completely unrelated to energy production, transmission, maintenance, or delivery. Excess revenues could/should probably be refunded annually to so-called members, if transparent reconciliation spotlighted and then eliminated internal expenditures for non-energy line items.
