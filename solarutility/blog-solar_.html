A Deeper Dive into Arizona Utility History and Solar Politics

"We didn't start the fire, it was always burning since solar's been churning..."

AZ Solar History Overview

Before You Invest:
Knowledge is only potential power. It becomes kinetic energy when applied—when you make smart, informed decisions.
With that said, Yes, you can still empower your roof. But first, you need to understand the politi-solar nuance. And yes, there's a catch: how much solar you're allowed to install, how your system is interconnected, and how it's forced to conform to draconian rate plans—all of it is dictated by a monopoly-driven framework. These topology-specific policies and pricing mandates can drive your annual savings down to net-zero—or worse.

This isn't just a theoretical risk. It's a real, ongoing erosion of value, fueled by a regulatory dumpster fire the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC) has never managed to get under control. Your potential for any level of energy independence remains largely dependent on your provider's benevolence, internal politics, and supervisory overreach. Which now includes the use of smart “spy meters”, behind their billing meters, that actually monitor your storage capacity and can be used to interpret your usage behaviors with unsettling precision.

Solar Was Supposed to Be Simple, Reliable and Economical: Back in 2009-2010, the original solar concept was elegantly straightforward. You produced a seasonally predictable amount of electricity on your roof. More during the longer days of summer, less in the shorter days of winter. Your home consumed what was needed while the sun was shining, and the excess went over to your neighbors place via the utility company street wiring. That excess energy traveled a very short hop in terms of utility distances, literally at the speed of light, where it was instantly resold by your friendly neighborhood single-source utility company, through the smart meter on someone else's house. The utility didn't generate that power. They didn't even move it very far. They just managed it by default with wires already buried in place that handled the relatively small amount of surplus. It was a wash for them, and therein lies their problem.

Maybe your unused energy helped a neighbor heat some water, do some laundry or kept the stay-at-home parent and the kids cool while you were at work. It is a clean, efficient seamless exchange. Everyone benefits....
The utility company has less demand on their mid-day production resources, as they move (resell) your excess energy across the street, to which they had no expense or effort in relation to production.
Fast forward to 2013-2014. Utilities began to panic over the accelerating deployment of the private solar ownership model. Behind the scenes, they are scrambling in their back-offices and lobbying at the AZCC to preserve declining revenue. The net result fuel to the dumpster fire? Higher connection fees. More charges and surcharges. Export limitations. Outright denials of compensation. Diminishing buyback tariffs. And yes, those smart-spy-meter deployments that track energy intake going toward your private storage container and the corresponding discharge usage.

Utilities began applying commercial tactics that track lower level demand charges at even less than 10kW, and then applied punitive charges toward residential customers who dared to go solar. The goal was clear: encumber and inhibit the simple system processing and replace it with a confusing, anti-competitive, revenue recovery maze. The concept of decentralizing sustainable solar energy was put back on the shelf. Don't misunderstand; utility companies value solar, just not you your roof.

Arizona's Energy Landscape is a Profitability-Driven model and is dominated by three major players: APS (Pinnacle West), SRP, and TEP (UniSource). These aren't just power producers and distributors, they are energy policy architects that keep tabs on what the other providers are getting away with. Most are nominally overseen by the AZCC, but SRP operates under its own antiquated governance model, entirely outside ACC visibility, reporting or jurisdiction.

SRP, a pseudo-municipal "not-for-profit" entity, has deployed solar rate plans so punitive they should deter any rational prospect from pursuing sustainable alternative energy. Their internal politics and policy decisions are designed to funnel 100% of your energy expenditure back into their system whether they centrally produced the power, or not. SRP's solar customer billing behaviors and revenue processing “quack like a corporate duck.” They propagated the infamous “duck curve” based on the technical difficulties that Hawaiian slum-lord utilities were having, in order to justify their obsession with revenue retention, which is code for "profitability". Internal memos allegedly boast about how lucrative their favorite demand-focused solar rate plan is. Surplus revenue is easily absorbed by SRP for some very nice amenity facilities that are unrelated to energy production, transmission, maintenance, or delivery. Excess revenues could probably be refunded annually, to so-called members if transparent reconciliation excluded expenditures on non-energy related line items.

What does this mean for you? Whether you're just starting your solar analysis or rethinking past purchase decisions, understanding Solar Rates, Policies, and Politics (SRPP) is essential to making Smart Energy Management (SEM) decisions.
