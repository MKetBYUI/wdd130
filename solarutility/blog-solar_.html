AZ Solar History Overview

While conspiracy, anti-trust and collusion are usually subtle, concealed and quiet, it doesn't mean that something is not going on. Arizona's Energy Landscape is influenced by a revenue-profitability-driven model that is dominated by three major players: Pinnacle West (APS), UniSource (TEP) and of-course NFP-SRP. These aren't just power producers and distributors, they are energy policy architects that keep close tabs on what the other non-competitive providers are getting away with. Most of the Arizona energy producers and distributors are nominally overseen by the AZCC, but SRP along with other not-for-profit NFP's operate "behind" antiquated governance models, entirely outside of AZCC visibility (reporting) or jurisdiction.

Solar Was Supposed to Be Simple, Reliable and Economical: Back in 2009 - 2010, the original solar concept was elegantly straightforward. You produced a seasonally predictable amount of electricity on your roof. More during the longer days of summer, less in the shorter days of winter. You set your thermostat at a preferred, comfortable temperature for your home and left for work. The systems in the home, including parasite loads like clocks, internet, garage door opener and device chargers, consumed what they needed while the sun was shining. Excess energy went backward through your meter and slid over to your neighbors place via the utility company street wiring. Maybe your unused energy helped a neighbor heat some water, cook some food, do some laundry, or kept the non-typical stay-at-home parent and the kids cool while you were at work. It was a clean, efficient seamless exchange. Everyone benefited.
The utility company had less demand on their mid-day production resources, as they moved (resold) your (free to them) excess energy across the street. To which they had zero expense or effort in relation to the production, transmission, or expense of transfer. That excess energy traveled a very short hop in terms of utility distances, literally at the speed of light, where it was instantly resold by your friendly, neighborhood, single-source utility company. It went right through their bi-directional smart meter into someone else's house and AC unit. No harm, No foul. The utility didn't generate that power. They didn't even move it very far. They just managed it organically, with wires already buried in place that handled your relatively small amount of energy surplus without much effort. It was a wash for them, (and therein lies their problem).

Fast forward to 2013-2014. Utilities are in full panic mode over the accelerating deployment of the shared-energy, private-ownership model. Behind the scenes they are screaming hysterically about the "Duck Curve", pointing to the mess that massive solar adoption in Hawaii was causing for their antiquated, unkept and overworked grid structure that still had/has WW2 cobwebs hanging off of their under-equipped, low-tech, slumlord system. Meanwhile, they are strategizing and developing more rhetoric in their back-offices, while soliciting the AZCC to stop the bleeding and to help recover diverted revenue. The net lobbying result was Full Ignition of the AZCC Solar Dumpster Fire. Higher "connection fees", more charges, surcharges and weird "taxes" that no-one voted for, or understands, system limitations, export limitations, diminishing buyback tariffs, and more recently there are outright denials of any compensation with various electric delivery providers. And don't forget, those smart-spy-meter deployments that track energy intake going toward your private storage container, that are also providing visibility that will help quantify your corresponding energy time-window habits, that are essential toward developing their future rate plan strategies.

Utilities can and do apply punitive commercial tactics that track lower level demand charges at even less than 10kW, and then leverage punitive charges against residential customers who dare to go solar. The goals are very clear: Discourage access, Encumber and Inhibit all simple-system processing, to be replaced with a hyper-confusing, anti-competitive, Revenue Recovery Rat Maze (RRRM). The concept of decentralized, sustainable solar energy savings is done. If not permanently "deep six'd", it is for sure buried in a back-room safe. Please don't misunderstand; Utility companies absolutely value solar, but they also Love to Hate it on your roof.

SRP, a pseudo-municipal-NFP "not-for-profit" entity, has deployed solar rate plans so punitive they should deter any intelligent, rational solar prospect from pursuing sustainable energy alternatives. Their internal politics and policy decisions are designed to funnel 100% of your energy expenditure back into their system whether they centrally produced the power, or not. SRP's solar customer billing behaviors and revenue processing “quack like a corporate duck.”. They helped propagate the infamous “duck curve” hysteria based on the technical difficulties that Hawaiian slum-lord utilities were having with their 1950's architecture, in order to justify their obsession with revenue retention, which is NFP code for an accounting principle known as "profitability". Internal memos allegedly boast about how lucrative their favorite demand-based solar rate plan is. Surplus revenue is easily absorbed by SRP for some very nice employee amenity facilities that are completely unrelated to energy production, transmission, maintenance, or delivery. Excess revenues could/should probably be refunded annually to so-called members, if transparent reconciliation highlighted and then eliminated internal expenditures on non-energy line items.

What does this mean for you? Whether you're just starting your solar analysis or rethinking past purchase decisions, understanding Solar Rates, Policies, and Politics (SRPP) is essential to making Smart Energy Management (SEM) decisions.
