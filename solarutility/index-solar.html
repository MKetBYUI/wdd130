Arizona Solar Regulation Timeline

Arizona has played a pivotal role in shaping solar policy in the U.S. The Arizona Corporation Commission (AZCC) has been the hub of regulatory mandates that affect private solar ownership and corporate revenue streams. This is a consolidated timeline of key developments:

2006 – Renewable Energy Standard and Tariff (REST)

The AZCC approved the REST rules, requiring regulated utilities to generate 15% of their energy from renewable sources by 2025. This policy laid the groundwork for utility-scale and distributed solar growth across the state.

2008 – Net Metering Mandated

Arizona formally adopted net metering, allowing solar customers to receive full retail credit for excess electricity sent back to the grid. This policy spurred rapid rooftop solar adoption and positioned Arizona as a national leader in solar installations.

2013 – Net Metering Fee Introduced

The AZCC approved a monthly fee for solar customers under APS, marking the beginning of a shift away from full retail compensation. This move was seen as a compromise between utilities and solar advocates, but it signaled growing tension.

2015–2017 – Net Metering Replaced by Export Rates

SRP abolished net metering in 2015, replacing it with a fixed export rate and demand-based billing. In 2017, the AZCC officially ended net metering for APS and TEP, adopting the Resource Comparison Proxy (RCP) model, which compensates solar exports at a reduced, annually adjusted rate.

2020s – Ongoing Adjustments and Advocacy

Export rates continue to decline annually, and solar customers must now navigate complex rate plans, time-of-use pricing, and demand charges. Advocacy efforts persist to ensure fair compensation and grid access for distributed solar users.
