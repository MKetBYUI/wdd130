Arizona Solar Regulation Timeline

Arizona has played an interesting role in shaping solar policy in the U.S. The Arizona Corporation Commission (AZCC) has been the hub of regulatory mandates that affect private solar deployments and corporate revenue streams. This page is a consolidated timeline of some important AZCC and Utility processing:

2006 Renewable Energy Standard and Tariff (REST)

The AZCC approved the REST rules, requiring regulated utilities to generate 15% of their energy from renewable sources by 2025. This policy put the spurs into utility-scale and distributed solar access across the state.

2008 Net Metering Reimbursement Mandated for Retail Solar Producers

Arizona formally adopted Net-Metering as a very popular consumer satisfication concept, that ultimately became a virtual cigarette flicked into a virtual dumpster full of shreded cardboard, oily rags and old tires. Mandating that solar adoptees receive full retail credit for any excess energy sent across, or down the street to their neighbors, (through the utility grid wiring) became an issue that utility providers had to take issue with, or at least ask "Hey, We are hosting this transfer and billing the transferee at zero net profit, so what is in this exchange us?". Hindsight really is 20/15 and in fairness to the AZCC Commisioners, there would have been zero foresight into the magnitude of decentralizing energy transfer and revenue diversion just a few years later. Bottom line: This single policy mandate spun up rooftop solar adoption at private residences, public facilities and business venues at an unprecedented rate.

2013 Net Metering Fee Introduced

The AZCC approved a monthly punitive fee for solar customers in APS territory, marking the beginning of the shift away from full retail price compensation. This move was spun as a compromise between utilities and solar advocates, but it benchmarked the turning point of growing tension, consumer resentment for a full-reverse pull-back, and future provider backlash against what should have been an equitable Kum-ba-ya retreat with s'mores, instead of a greasy rag dumpster fire.

2015-2017 Net Metering Replaced by Export Rates

Unaffected and unhindered by the AZCC, SRP effectively abolished Net-Metering for their members in 2015, replacing it with fixed export rates and punitive demand-based charges, all to be unilaterally determined by internal board processing. In 2017, the AZCC officially ended net metering mandates for APS and TEP, who anxiously adopted into Resource Comparison Proxy (RCP) models, which compensate excess solar exports at annually adjusted rates.

2020s Ongoing Adjustments and Advocacy

Export rates continue to decline annually, and solar customers must navigate complex rate plans, high time-of-use TOU pricing, and punitive demand charges. Advocacy efforts persist to reset the table with fair compensation, non-punitive rate plans and normal-hours grid access like non-solar consumers enjoy.

Understanding this regulatory history is valuable for setting expectations toward evaluating a potential investment in solar energy production.
