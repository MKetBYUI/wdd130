Arizona Solar Regulation Timeline

Arizona and Hawaii of all places, have played interesting roles in shaping solar policy across the U.S. The Arizona Corporation Commission (AZCC) has been a hub for regulatory mandates that affect private solar deployments and corporate revenue streams. This page is a consolidated timeline of some important AZCC and Utility processing that you should be aware of:

2006 Renewable Energy Standard and Tariff (REST)

The AZCC approved the REST rules, requiring regulated utilities to generate 15% of their energy from renewable sources by 2025. This policy put the spurs into utility-scale and distributed solar access across the state as similar regulation and legislation rolled out across the sun-belt states.

2008 Net Metering Reimbursement Mandated for Retail Solar Producers

Arizona formally adopted Net-Metering as a very popular consumer satisfaction concept, that ultimately became a virtual cigarette flicked into a virtual dumpster full of shredded cardboard, oily rags and old tires.
When the AZCC mandated that private solar system owners (I am one of those owners), receive full retail billing credit (I am also one of those beneficiaries), for any excess energy they sent out to neighbors that are down or across the street, (through utility grid wiring), an issue was created that utility providers would take issue with in the not to distant future.

2010 - 2012 Net Metering creates an avalanche of deployments

I imagine the dialog with the AZCC probably went something like this:
"Hey Guys, We didn't generate the energy, but we are giving up lots of revenue for the privilege of hosting this transfer, and we are processing the billing at nearly net-zero profit. so what is in the deal for us?" Hindsight really is 20-15 and in fairness to the AZCC Commission, they are elected politicians, and there would have been "net-zero" foresight into the incoming problem associated with decentralizing energy transfer for a bunch of corporate types that have operated inside of their non-competitive bubbles since their reliably air-tight revenue-worlds were created.

2013 Net Metering Fee Introduced

The AZCC approved a monthly punitive fee for solar customers in APS territory, marking the beginning of the shift away from full retail price compensation. This move was spun as a compromise between utilities and solar advocates, but it benchmarked the turning point toward growing tension, consumer resentment for a full-reverse pull-back, and hostile provider backlash against what should have been set up initially as an equitable Kum-ba-ya retreat with s'mores, instead of a greasy rag + tire fire.

2015-2017 Net Metering Replaced by Export Rates

Unaffected and unhindered by the AZCC, SRP effectively abolished Net-Metering for their members in 2015, replacing it with fixed export rates and/or punitive demand-based charges, all to be unilaterally determined by internal board processing. In 2017, the AZCC officially ended net metering mandates for regulated utilities like APS and TEP, who instantly adopted into Resource Comparison Proxy (RCP) models, which compensate excess solar exports at annually adjusted rates based on other sources of wholesale energy and the associated system costs.

2020s Ongoing Adjustments and Advocacy

Export rates continue to decline annually, solar customers must navigate complex rate plans, high TOU price plans, and punitive demand charges. Advocacy efforts persist to reset the table with fair compensation, non-punitive rate plans and normal-hours of affordable grid access that anti-solar consumers enjoy.

Understanding this regulatory history is valuable for setting expectations toward evaluating a potential investment in privately owned solar production.
